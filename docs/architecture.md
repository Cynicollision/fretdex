# Architecture

Fretdex is a static web app with no backend: a SvelteKit site built with `adapter-static`, TypeScript throughout, with the music theory and the audio in framework-free packages so they can be reused (the owner's synth-toy projects) and tested on their own. Everything runs in the browser; only settings, pins and recents are stored, and only on the device.

The repository layout, tech stack, tooling, hosting and styling below are built (M01 T1 to T3), and so are the theory engine's pitch, spelling and tuning model (T4) storage, settings and the context bar (T5), and the app shell and URLs (T6); the rest describes the intended design. When code proves part of it wrong, fix this file in the same PR.

## Layers

```
 Views (SvelteKit routes)          one route per card; state in the URL
   │ current context (key, instrument, tuning, capo, labels…)
   ▼
 Theory engine (fretkit)           notes, intervals, chords, scales, keys, modes,
   │ notes · positions · voicings    roman numerals, fretboard mapping, positions,
   │                                 voicing search
   ├──────────────► Diagrams        Svelte SVG components: Fretboard, ChordBox,
   │                                 Keyboard, Circle, Staff
   └──────────────► Audio engine    Web Audio voices and a scheduler; later the
                                     tuner's pitch detection
 Storage (IndexedDB)               settings, pins, recents, hidden voicings
```

A view reads the current context and its own URL, asks the theory engine for notes, positions and voicings, and hands them to diagrams to draw and to the audio engine to play. Diagrams and audio never compute theory themselves, so a wrong note is always a `fretkit` bug with a unit test to write.

## Repository layout

npm workspaces in one repository (M01, decision 8):

| Folder | Package | What | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| `app/` | `fretdex` | The SvelteKit app: routes, diagrams, stores, storage, PWA | `fretkit`, `audio` |
| `packages/fretkit/` | `@fretdex/fretkit` | Theory engine: wraps Tonal.js and adds tunings, fretboard mapping, positions, voicing search and the chord-voicing dataset | Tonal.js |
| `packages/audio/` | `@fretdex/audio` | Audio engine: voices (plucked string, bass, electric piano), strum and sequence scheduling, click and metronome, master volume; later pitch detection for the tuner | Web Audio API only |

Neither package imports Svelte or touches the DOM beyond Web Audio (the app opens the microphone and hands the audio package its stream), so both run under Vitest without a browser.

The packages have no build step: each exports its TypeScript source (`"exports": { ".": "./src/index.ts" }`), and the app's Vite compiles it with the app. That's enough while they're unpublished (M01, decision 9); publishing them for the synth toys would add a build to `dist/`. The app is SvelteKit 3, whose configuration lives in the `sveltekit()` plugin's options in `app/vite.config.ts` (there's no `svelte.config.js`), and the root holds the shared tooling: Prettier, ESLint and the base `tsconfig.base.json` the packages extend.

## The theory engine (`fretkit`)

- **Pitch and spelling.** Every note is spelled, not just a pitch class: F# major contains E#, not F (THY). A note is a string in Tonal.js's form (`F#`, `Bbb`, `E2`), read from what users type and URLs carry by `parseNote` and shown by `noteLabel` (`note.ts`). `SCALE_TYPES` (`scale.ts`) lists every scale by its intervals, and `spellScale` spells one from its root through Tonal.js. `root.ts` decides which spelling a root takes from the accidentals setting (CTX-1.6): on Auto, by the key signature each scale is read in (`signatureFrom`). Spelling every scale on every root is the golden table, `packages/fretkit/golden/scales.md`.
- **Tuning model.** A tuning is an ordered list of open-string pitches, lowest string first, with octaves (`E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4`), at the pitch they sound (`TUNINGS` in `tuning.ts`). Every fretboard computation takes a tuning, so other string counts are a data change. Frets are counted from the nut with or without a capo: a capo only rules out the frets below it, and `openStrings` gives what the open strings sound with it (THY-3.5), while chord shapes are computed against the open strings and named by shape (CTX-3).
- **Fretboard mapping.** `mapScale` (`fretboard.ts`) lists every fret in a range where a scale's notes fall, on any tuning and capo, each with its note spelled as the scale spells it, its octave, its interval from the root and its degree.
- **Modes.** `mode.ts` knows the seven modes of the major scale in brightness order (`BRIGHTNESS`), each one's degree in its parent and characteristic note (`modeInfo`), its parent's root (`parentRoot`), and the triads and 7th chords on each degree with roman numerals relative to the mode (`diatonicChords`), computed from the scale's own notes. `stepFormula` writes any scale's steps (`W W H W W W H`).
- **Positions.** `scalePositions` (`position.ts`) gives the positions a scale card offers on an instrument, tuning and capo, grouped by kind, each with its number, its frets and its cells (as `mapScale` spells them). Every position is computed, not stored: CAGED positions are five-fret windows placed from the major root's fret on the lowest string, each pitch kept once on the higher of two strings, and a mode or natural minor takes its parent major's (SCL-3.1); pentatonic boxes and 3-notes-per-string patterns climb the scale two or three notes a string from each note on the lowest string (SCL-3.2, SCL-3.3), the blues scales adding their blue note within the box; bass boxes climb one octave one finger a fret, and two octaves as that box twice with a shift to the next string (SCL-4.1). CAGED positions and boxes need standard guitar tuning, matched by pitch (SCL-3.6), and a capo moves everything up by working from the open strings it sounds. The golden table `golden/positions.md` lists every position of the shapes the others reuse on all 12 roots.
- **Voicings.** A chord's voicings come from the curated dataset (standard tuning) and from a voicing search over the fretboard for families (drop 2, drop 3, shells) and for alternate tunings, with playability limits (fret span, open strings, muted strings).
- **Chord identification.** Given frets in a tuning, every chord name the notes fit, simplest first, inversions and slash chords included (Identify, CHD-6).
- **Progressions.** A roman-numeral library, resolved to chords in a key (or mode) by Tonal.js.

## Routes and the URL scheme

Paths name the thing; query parameters hold view options. Global settings stay out of the URL unless a view overrides them (APP-3).

| Route | Example | Query options |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | Home board, with the modules under it while nothing is pinned (PIN-2.5) | — |
| `/chords`, `/chords/[root]/[quality]` | `/chords/A/m7b5` | `v` voicing, `inv` inversion |
| `/chords/identify` | `/chords/identify?f=x32010` | `f` frets, lowest string first |
| `/scales`, `/scales/[root]/[scale]` | `/scales/G/major` | the list: `root`; the card: one of `pos` 1–5 (CAGED or pentatonic box), `nps` 1–7 or `box` 1–4 (bass), `deg` highlighted degrees (`135`), `labels` |
| `/triads`, `/triads/[root]/[quality]` | `/triads/C/major` | `set` 1–4 (all four when absent), `inv` |
| `/modes`, `/modes/[root]` | `/modes/Db`, the modes of D♭ major (a mode's card is its scale card, `/scales/Fs/dorian`) | `labels` |
| `/keys/[key]` (`/keys` redirects to `/circle`) | `/keys/Bb-major` | `sevenths` |
| `/circle` | Circle of fifths | `key` |
| `/progressions`, `/progressions/[slug]` | `/progressions/ii-v-i` | `key`, `bpm` |
| `/settings` | Settings, export and import | — |
| `/tuner` (M04) | Tuner | — |
| `/metronome` (M04) | `/metronome?bpm=132&sig=6-8` | `bpm`, `sig`, `sub` |

Any route may also carry `inst`, `tuning` and `capo`, so a pin keeps its own instrument, tuning and capo whatever the global setting is. A built-in tuning is written by its id (`tuning=drop-d`) and a custom one as its pitches (`tuning=D2-A2-D3-G3-A3-D4`), so its URL works on a device that hasn't saved it; pitches with no `inst` give the instrument with that many strings. Sharps are written `s` in paths (`Fs`); `%23` is accepted too. Each module's list route takes the same filters its list offers (for example `/chords?key=E-minor`).

The routes of M01 are built: the Scales list and scale card (`ScaleCard.svelte`), and the Modes overview (`ModesOverview.svelte`), which `/modes` shows for the current key's parent major and `/modes/[root]` for the major key in its path. The overview of a key is a `Card` of kind `modes`, whose one option is `labels`, drawn from the fallback page as the cards are, and its back control goes Home. On `/modes`, which is prerendered, choosing labels moves to the key's own path, as only a card route keeps options. Everything about reading and writing URLs is in `app/src/lib/url/`:

- **Cards.** `routes.ts` reads a card's path into a `Card` (`readScaleCard` and the like), writes it back (`cardPath`) and names it (`cardTitle`); `href.ts` gives a card's link through SvelteKit's `resolve`. A card route's `load` (`cardLoad` in `load.ts`) shows Not found for a path it can't read (`+error.svelte`, APP-3.5), and redirects any other spelling to the one the app writes (`%23` and `#` to `s`). Any spelling of a root is read, so a linked root opens as written (THY-2.5).
- **Rendering.** Home, Settings and the module lists are prerendered. Cards aren't, as every root makes hundreds of them: a card route sets `prerender` and `ssr` off, and is drawn in the browser from `adapter-static`'s fallback page, `404.html`, which GitHub Pages serves for any path it has no file for (with a 404 status, which browsers ignore). A card therefore blinks blank on a first load until the app's JavaScript runs.
- **Query.** `readViewOptions` reads a card's options (`pos`, `nps`, `box`, `deg`, `labels`; one position option at most, and choosing one clears the others), `readListRoot` the Scales list's root, and `context.ts` the override (`readOverride`). A prerendered list reads its query only once it has mounted, as prerendering has none. Once the page has hydrated and the settings have loaded, the root layout rewrites the query as the app would write it (`canonicalQuery`, with the list's filter for the routes in `LIST_ROUTES`), so an option it can't read, or doesn't know, leaves the URL. Changing an option replaces the history entry (`setViewOption` in `navigate.ts`, APP-3.2).
- **The override** shows in the context bar, which offers to clear it (APP-2.3); views read `viewContext`, the override where there is one and the settings elsewhere.
- **Back.** `BackLink.svelte` goes back in the browser's history when the previous entry is one of the app's (the Navigation API's `canGoBack`, or else any history at all), and otherwise follows its link, the card's module list (APP-1.3).
- **The wake lock** (`wake-lock.ts`) is held while **Keep screen awake** is on and taken again each time the app is shown, as the browser drops it when the app is hidden (APP-5.5).

The root layout puts the context bar (`ContextBar.svelte` in `app/src/lib/components/`) at the top of every route: ☰, then the context (on a card, without the key, `card` in `ContextBar.svelte`), which opens its sheet. There's no bottom bar (APP-1.1). ☰ opens the menu (`MenuSheet.svelte`, a `Sheet`): Home, the modules and tools as rows in `MODULES` and `TOOLS`'s order, those not built yet dimmed in their places, then Settings; the screen open, or the module a card is in, is marked, and following a row closes it. The context sheet (`ContextSheet.svelte`) sets the key, instrument, tuning and capo straight into the settings, so whatever screen is open follows at once (APP-2.2), and holds the master volume and mute (AUD-1.2); custom tunings are entered there (`TuningEditor.svelte`) and managed on Settings.

## Storage

IndexedDB, through `idb-keyval` (`idbStore` in `app/src/lib/storage/idb.ts`: a `records` object store in a `fretdex` database), holds five things: settings, custom tunings, pins (with groups and order), recents (the last 20 views) and hidden voicings. So far settings, custom tunings and pins are stored. Nothing leaves the device.

- **Versioned records.** Each thing is one record, stored with its format's version (`loadRecord` and `saveRecord` in `storage/record.ts`). Loading migrates an older record forward a step at a time, then reads it field by field, so anything missing or invalid takes its default rather than stopping the app. A record written by a newer version (a tab not yet updated) is read as far as this version understands it and never saved over. If IndexedDB is unavailable, or a migration can't read a record, the app runs on its defaults; if a save fails, the change still applies until the app closes.
- **Settings.** `Settings` and its record format, `SETTINGS_FORMAT`, are in `settings/settings.ts`, which also switches instrument (CTX-2.1) and matches a tuning to a built-in one by its pitches, however they're spelled (`builtInTuning`). The selected tuning is stored as its pitches, not a name. `SettingsStore` (`settings/store.svelte.ts`) holds them as runes state: the `settings` it exports loads them once asked (`ready`) and saves every change, and a change made before they've loaded waits for them (a change that depends on another setting is given as a function of the settings, so it reads them as loaded). Views read `settings.current`, which holds the defaults until the stored settings arrive.
- **Custom tunings** are a second record, `customTunings` (`CUSTOM_TUNINGS_FORMAT` in `settings/tunings.ts`): each instrument's saved tunings, up to 10, each with an id that outlasts renames, a name and its pitches. `CustomTuningsStore` (`settings/tunings.svelte.ts`) works as `SettingsStore` does. A tuning's name is a built-in one's, else a saved one's with the same pitches, else "Custom" (`tuningName`), so deleting the tuning in use leaves it in use as "Custom".
- **Pins** are a third record, `pins` (`PINS_FORMAT` in `pins/pins.ts`, version 2): the board's groups in order, each an id that outlasts renames and moves and a name unique ignoring case, and its pins in the board's order, the ungrouped ones first and then each group's (`readPinBoard` keeps them so), each an id, a label, the view's URL, a path and query as the app writes them, and the id of its group if it has one. Version 1 had no groups; its migration adds none. The board's changes (`placePin`, `movePin`, `addGroup`, `renameGroup`, `placeGroup`, `moveGroup`, `deleteGroup`) are plain functions there. A pin is the URL, not the card's parts, so a view gains options without the record changing, and a pin a newer version wrote for a route this one lacks is kept, its tile showing just its label. `pinTarget` writes a view's pin, adding the settings' instrument, tuning and capo where the URL has none and they aren't guitar, Standard, no capo (PIN-1.4); `pinIdentity` says what a pin is of, a card's path or a list's path and filter, which the Pin button matches by (PIN-1.2); and `pinTile` reads back what its tile draws. `PinsStore` (`pins/store.svelte.ts`) works as `SettingsStore` does, and asks for persistent storage when the first pin goes on an empty board (PIN-6.2). Home's board (`PinBoard.svelte`) drags a tile or a group's heading once it's held for half a second (`BoardDrag` in `pins/board-drag.svelte.ts`, PIN-2.3): until then a move is the page's scroll and a release the tile's tap. What's dragged stays in place, faded, a copy follows the finger, and a bar in a gap or an outline round an empty group marks where it would land, so nothing on the page moves under the finger; with nothing ungrouped, an Ungrouped area below the groups takes a pin out of its group. A press on a ⋯ button is left to the button, and a drag ends without dropping if the window loses focus or Home goes away (`cancel`). `pins/drag.ts` works out the place from the tiles' and groups' boxes.
- **Backup** (PIN-5, `backup/backup.ts`) is a JSON file of every record as it's stored, its version beside its data, under the record's key. Reading one loads each record through `loadRecord` from a store over the file, so an import is migrated and checked exactly as loading from the device is; a file a newer version wrote (including one with a record this version doesn't keep), or one with a record missing or damaged, is refused. **Add to what's here** merges with `addPins` (skipping pins already pinned by URL, matching groups with `groupNamed`, giving what it adds new ids) and `addTunings` (skipping what `cannotSave` would refuse); **Replace everything** hands each store the file's record. Settings' Backup section (`routes/settings/Backup.svelte`) does the file's saving and picking.
- **Loading after hydration.** Home, Settings and the lists are prerendered with the defaults, and Svelte keeps a prerendered page's text as it was when it hydrates it, so stored values that arrived before hydration would never show. So the stores load nothing until asked: the root layout calls `ready()` on each in `onMount`, once the page has hydrated, and the stored values then arrive as an ordinary change. The context bar stays blank until they have, rather than showing the defaults.
- **The theme** is also copied into `localStorage` (`applyTheme` in `settings/theme.ts`), because IndexedDB can't be read before the first paint: a script in `app.html` sets `data-theme` from the copy, so a Light or High contrast theme shows from the first frame. The root layout then applies the stored theme and sets the `theme-color` meta to the theme's `--bg`.
- **Persistent storage.** Running installed, and at the first pin, the app asks the browser to keep its storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`, in `storage/persist.ts`), and Settings says whether it did (PIN-6.2).

## Offline and install

The service worker precaches the whole build (app, voicing dataset, icons, fonts), so after the first load the app needs no network. When the app opens with a network and a new version has been deployed, the new service worker installs in the background, takes over and reloads the app (`registerType: 'autoUpdate'`), so a launch picks up the new version within seconds. The root layout (`src/routes/+layout.svelte`) registers the service worker and adds the manifest's link. A navigation the service worker hasn't precached, such as any card, gets the fallback page, `404.html`, which is precached too (`kit.adapterFallback` and `spa` in `app/vite.config.ts`), so every route opens offline. The manifest gives the app its name, icon and theme colors. The app is served from the root of its own subdomain, so it needs no base path and the service worker's scope is the whole origin.

## Styling

- **Tokens.** `app/src/lib/styles/tokens.css` holds every colour, the font stack, the type scale (14, 16, 18, 22 and 28px, as `rem` so the system text size applies) and the 44px tap target. `:root` is the Dark theme; `data-theme="light"` or `"contrast"` on `<html>` swaps the colours, set from the theme setting (CTX-1.8, [Storage](#storage)). The diagram colours are tokens too (`--neck`, `--fret`, `--note`, `--root` and so on), so a theme repaints the diagrams. `tokens.spec.ts` checks every text pair's contrast against APP-6.2.
- **Fonts.** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next comes from its Fontsource package, imported in the root layout. It has no musical signs, and no text font checked had 𝄪 and 𝄫, so `fonts.css` adds Fretdex Accidentals: Noto Music's ♭ ♮ ♯ 𝄪 𝄫, raised to sit beside capitals and given Atkinson's line metrics, with a `unicode-range` of just those five. It's made by `app/scripts/make-accidentals-font.py` and committed. Both are bundled into `_app/` (the accidentals inlined in the CSS) and precached.
- **The look.** A charcoal page, a rosewood neck with nickel frets, bone dots and an amber root in Dark; a maple neck with dark dots in Light; black, white and yellow in High contrast. The root is a square and other notes circles (DIAG-1.7), and frets narrow gently up the neck (DIAG-1.8).
- **Diagrams** are SVG drawn by Svelte components in `app/src/lib/components/`, laid out by plain functions in `app/src/lib/diagrams/` that the `logic` tests check. A diagram takes a `NoteSet` (a scale, a chord's tones from `noteSet`, or a lone note) and asks `fretkit` where it falls (`mapScale`, `mapKeyboard`); it never works out a note itself. Each has a visually hidden caption as its text alternative (APP-6.4).
  - `Fretboard.svelte`: strings 44 apart, the lowest at the bottom, with an open-strings column 40 wide left of a 7-wide nut; dots 30 across with 14px labels; inlays, and fret numbers below the neck at the inlay frets. Each fret is 0.98 the width of the one below (`fretWidths`), easing toward even rather than go under the 44px tap target. When the whole fret range fits at that width, as on a phone turned sideways, it's all shown; otherwise, as upright, a stretch of up to six frets with Lower and Higher, over `NeckStrip.svelte`, the whole range in small with its roots, which moves the stretch where it's tapped (DIAG-1.10). Which one is decided by the width available, not the phone's orientation, so a wide window shows the whole neck too; a card's `Screen` is `wide` (72rem) to give it room. Left-handed, every x is mirrored and nothing else changes, so labels stay upright (DIAG-1.5). A capo is a bar in `--nut` across its fret, the frets below it dimmed and empty, and the notes it frets drawn on it (DIAG-1.9). Given a position (`focus`), its notes are at full strength and the rest dimmed, the neck widens past the fret range to hold it, and upright it shows the position's frets, with Lower and Higher only for one wider than a stretch; the scale card steps between positions itself (`ScaleCard.svelte`, with the names from `app/src/lib/scales/positions.ts`). A tap is reported with the fret, its pitch and its note, spelled as a lone note when the set hasn't one (`ontap`, DIAG-1.6).
  - `Keyboard.svelte`: two octaves from C3, a white key a fourteenth of the width and black keys 0.6 as wide and 0.62 as tall. A key's mark is in the other key colour (`--key-black` on white keys, `--key-white` on black) so it shows in every theme. Small in a card; tapped, it fills the width with labels in two rows, black keys' above white keys', so neighbours never collide (DIAG-3.1).
  - `FretboardMini.svelte`: a pin tile's fretboard, 150 by 72 or narrower to fit its tile: hairline frets and strings, the nut when fret 0 is in range, and dots 8 across without labels, the root a 9 square (DIAG-5.1). A pinned position shows its frets and only its notes; the full neck shows an octave from the capo (`pinTile`).
  - `ModeNeck.svelte`: a Modes overview row's neck, laid out by `layModeNeck` (`diagrams/mode-neck.ts`) with strings 23 apart and 22px dots, and not tappable, as its row is a link. It shows one position's frets, the mode's 3-notes-per-string pattern from its root on the lowest string, or a bass box (`rowPosition` in `app/src/lib/modes/overview.ts`, from `scalePositions`), with the scale's other notes there faded and the characteristic note ringed as on the scale card (MODE-1.12, MODE-1.13). The rows, their numerals and their slice of the step strip (`STEP_STRIP`, `stepSlice`) come from the same file.
- **Home** (`app/src/routes/+page.svelte`) is the pin board, `PinBoard.svelte`, alone, or with nothing pinned how to pin over the modules' grid (`ModuleGrid.svelte`). The board's tiles are about 170 wide, two across a phone, each a `--surface` box with a `--line` border and the `--radius`, its mini fretboard, its label (16, bold) and up to two lines in 14 `--muted`, the position or a list's filter, and the instrument, tuning and capo where they differ from the settings (PIN-2.2, PIN-2.6). A ⋯ button in each tile's corner opens its menu, a `Sheet.svelte` (a small bottom sheet, the context sheet's look) with Rename, the Group choice and New group…, Move up, Move down and Unpin. The ungrouped tiles come first, then each group under a heading (16, bold, over a `--line` rule) whose own ⋯ menu renames, moves and deletes it; an empty group says so. A card's or filtered list's Pin button (`PinButton.svelte`) sits beside its heading, in `Screen`'s `actions`, and opens a `Sheet` with Pin again and Unpin once it's pinned.
- **The icon** is `app/static/icon.svg`, a chord box in the Dark theme's colours; `app/scripts/draw-icons.ts` draws the PNGs from it.

## Audio

`@fretdex/audio` synthesizes everything; there are no sample files (AUD-7.2).

- **Voices** (`voices.ts`): `renderNote` renders a note of a voice into samples, by plain functions that the package's Node tests check for pitch, level and decay. Guitar is a Karplus–Strong plucked string: seeded noise, notched as a pick near the bridge does, round a delay line one period long, with an allpass making up the period's fraction of a sample so every fret is in tune, and a loss set so low strings ring longer. Bass is a sine with a saw's harmonics over it that fade faster the higher they are. The keyboard is a two-operator FM electric piano with a short tine. Each note is scaled to the same peak, with headroom for six strings ringing.
- **The engine** (`AudioEngine` in `engine.ts`) makes its `AudioContext` on the first sound, so nothing plays before a tap (AUD-1.1), and resumes it whenever a sound finds it suspended. Each rendered note is cached as an `AudioBuffer` by voice and MIDI note and played through its own gain, then the master gain (the volume squared, or 0 when muted) and a limiter. A note given a channel (`string:3`, `key:60`) cuts the one sounding on that channel with a 10 ms fade, or stops it before it starts if it's scheduled later, and leaves the rest ringing (AUD-1.3). `run` plays a track through the scheduler, its notes cutting each other by channel as taps do, and stops every other sound unless it's marked `under`, as the metronome will be; any new sound stops a run that isn't, including the notes still ringing after its track has ended, and tells it so (`onstop`) if its track hadn't. The silent-switch request below is repeated on every sound, in case the phone paused it in the background.
- **The scheduler** (`Scheduler` in `scheduler.ts`) runs everything that keeps time: scale runs, progression loops, the click and count-in, and the metronome. A timer wakes it every 25 ms and it hands over each event due in the next 100 ms with its time on the audio clock, worked out from its beat and the tempo's anchor rather than by adding up gaps, so a loop never drifts and a tempo change takes effect from the next beat. Events it wakes more than 50 ms late for, as after the tab slept, are skipped. Several tracks can play at once.
- **The iPhone silent switch** (`silent-switch.ts`): on the first sound the engine sets `navigator.audioSession.type` to `playback` where Safari has it (16.4 and later), which lets Web Audio play on silent; an older iPhone or iPad gets a silent WAV made in code, looped in a media element, which does the same (AUD-1.4).
- **In the app**, `app/src/lib/audio/sound.ts` holds the one engine, and `playFret` and `playKey`, which the cards' fretboard and keyboard call from their taps: a fret in the instrument's voice at the pitch it sounds, capo included (`fretMidi`, THY-4.1, CTX-3.5), on its string's channel, and a key in the electric piano on its own. The root layout keeps the engine's volume and mute in step with the settings. A scale card's **Play** runs the track `runNotes` and `runTrack` (`scales/run.ts`) make from the position's cells, or an octave of the full neck, each note on its string's channel; the card lights each note when the audio clock reaches its time, checked on every animation frame rather than timed by the page's clock, as the audio clock stands still while a phone starts audio on the first sound (AUD-3.2).

The tuner (M04) adds an input side: the app opens the microphone only on the tuner screen and passes its stream to the audio package, whose pitch detector runs on the phone (autocorrelation in an AudioWorklet). Nothing is recorded.
